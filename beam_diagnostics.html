03 — BEAM DIAGNOSTICS

Visualizing the extracted beam.

Visible-range CCD diagnostics

Visible-range CCD imaging is used to obtain spatial profiles of the extracted negative hydrogen ion beam. The diagnostic provides a non-invasive method for characterizing the transverse beam distribution during beam transport.

The measured profiles provide information about beam intensity distribution, spatial inhomogeneity and beam evolution along the transport line.

These experimental measurements also provide reference data for comparison with computational beam-transport and space-charge models.

OPTICAL TOMOGRAPHY

Looking beyond a single beam profile.

A major part of the experimental work involves the development of a visible-range optical tomography system for non-invasive characterization of photon-emitting plasma and beam-related phenomena.

Unlike conventional beam profiling, where a single projection provides information about the observed transverse distribution, optical tomography uses measurements from multiple viewing directions to reconstruct the spatial distribution of the emitting region.

The diagnostic combines line CCD cameras, pinhole optics and synchronized data acquisition. Each camera records the optical emission along a different line-of-sight, providing complementary projections of the plasma or beam-emitting region.

Multi-camera tomographic system

The experimental system consists of multiple line CCD cameras arranged around the measurement region. Pinhole optics are used to define the imaging geometry and project the photon-emitting region onto the CCD detectors.

Simultaneous acquisition of the different projections allows the measured optical emission to be treated as a tomographic dataset. The acquired signals are subsequently calibrated, processed and mapped to the corresponding projection geometry.

The system has been developed with emphasis on both the experimental hardware and the computational reconstruction pipeline, providing a complete measurement-to-reconstruction framework.

RECONSTRUCTION

From optical projections to spatial information.

The reconstruction framework is developed in Python using numerical and image-processing tools. Different reconstruction approaches are investigated to understand the influence of projection number, viewing geometry and experimental limitations.

The present framework includes Back Projection (BP), Filtered Back Projection (FBP) and iterative reconstruction using the Simultaneous Algebraic Reconstruction Technique (SART).

These methods provide complementary approaches for recovering the two-dimensional distribution from the measured line-of-sight projections.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

Towards higher-dimensional plasma diagnostics.

Further development of the system will focus on increasing the number of viewing directions, improving geometrical calibration and reconstruction accuracy, and extending the diagnostic towards time-resolved measurements.

The longer-term objective is to establish a more complete spatial and temporal characterization of photon-emitting plasma and beam-related phenomena using optical tomography.
