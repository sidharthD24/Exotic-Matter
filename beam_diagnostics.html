03 — BEAM DIAGNOSTICS

Visualizing the extracted beam.

BEAM PROFILING

Visible-range CCD diagnostics

Visible-range CCD imaging is used to obtain spatial profiles of the extracted negative hydrogen ion beam. The diagnostic provides a non-invasive method for observing the transverse beam distribution during beam transport.

The measured profiles provide information about beam intensity distribution, spatial inhomogeneity and the evolution of the beam along the transport line. These measurements are particularly useful for studying changes in beam behaviour under different extraction and acceleration conditions.

Experimental beam profiles also provide an important reference for comparison with computational beam transport and Particle-in-Cell simulations, enabling the role of space charge and other transport mechanisms to be investigated.

OPTICAL TOMOGRAPHIC DIAGNOSTICS

Reconstructing spatial structure from multiple views.

In addition to direct beam profiling, I am developing a visible-range optical tomography system for the non-invasive investigation of photon-emitting plasma and beam-related phenomena. The objective is to recover the internal spatial distribution of the emitting region from a set of line-of-sight measurements obtained from different viewing directions.

The system combines multiple line CCD cameras with pinhole imaging optics and simultaneous data acquisition. Each camera records a projection of the emitting region from a different angle, and the measured projections are subsequently processed using computational reconstruction algorithms to estimate the underlying two-dimensional emission distribution.

SYSTEM DEVELOPMENT

Multi-view optical tomography

The diagnostic system is based on multiple optical viewing channels positioned around the plasma region. Line CCD cameras are combined with pinhole optics to acquire line-integrated emission measurements from different viewing angles.

The camera system is designed for synchronized image acquisition, allowing projections from multiple directions to be recorded under comparable plasma conditions. The acquired data are calibrated, geometrically mapped and prepared for tomographic reconstruction.

A key aspect of this work is the development of the complete diagnostic methodology, including the optical geometry, camera configuration, data acquisition procedure and computational reconstruction pipeline.

Optical system

Pinhole imaging geometry is used to define the line-of-sight projection of the emitting plasma onto the detector.

02

Multi-camera acquisition

Multiple line CCD cameras provide projections from different viewing directions around the measurement region.

The measured signals are processed and geometrically mapped to obtain the projection data required for reconstruction.

Reconstruction

Computational algorithms are used to reconstruct the two-dimensional spatial distribution from the measured line-of-sight projections.

COMPUTATIONAL RECONSTRUCTION

From projections to spatial distributions.

The tomographic reconstruction problem involves recovering a two-dimensional emission distribution from a limited number of measured projections. Different reconstruction approaches are being explored to evaluate their performance for the available optical geometry and number of viewing directions.

The reconstruction framework includes direct back-projection methods, filtered back-projection and iterative techniques such as the Simultaneous Algebraic Reconstruction Technique. These approaches allow the influence of projection number, viewing geometry and measurement limitations to be investigated.

BP

Back Projection

Direct reconstruction by projecting the measured line-of-sight information back through the reconstruction domain.

FBP

Filtered Back Projection

Projection data are filtered before reconstruction to improve spatial resolution and reduce the blurring associated with direct back projection.

SART

Iterative Reconstruction

An iterative approach for refining the reconstructed distribution based on the agreement between calculated and experimentally measured projections.

ONGOING DEVELOPMENT

Towards time-resolved volumetric diagnostics.

The current work establishes the basis for multi-view optical reconstruction of plasma emission. Future development will focus on increasing the number of viewing directions, improving reconstruction accuracy and extending the diagnostic capability towards time-resolved measurements.

The longer-term objective is to investigate the possibility of reconstructing the temporal evolution and volumetric structure of plasma and beam-related emission in more complex experimental systems.
