WHO I AM

Research driven by plasma, particles and beam science.

I am a PhD researcher working in the field of plasma physics and accelerator-based technologies, with a particular focus on negative hydrogen ion sources and neutral beam systems.

My research involves understanding how hydrogen plasma produces negative ions, how these ions are extracted and accelerated, and how their collective behaviour influences beam transport. I work across both experimental and computational approaches, combining diagnostics, numerical modelling and physical analysis to understand complex beam phenomena.

A central part of my work is the study of space-charge effects in negative-ion beams and their influence on beam divergence, inhomogeneity and transport through accelerator systems.

RESEARCH

From plasma production to beam transport

My research brings together plasma physics, accelerator science, diagnostics and computational modelling to study the complete evolution of negative-ion beams.

01

Negative Ion Sources

Experimental studies of hydrogen plasma and H− production in RF-driven negative ion sources, including plasma characteristics, extraction conditions and electron filtering.

02

Beam Extraction

Investigation of multi-aperture three-grid accelerator systems and the extraction of negative hydrogen ion beams, with emphasis on beamlet formation and transport.

03

Beam Transport

Study of beam divergence, beam inhomogeneity and collective space-charge interactions during propagation through accelerator beamlines.

04

Diagnostics

Development of optical diagnostics for characterizing plasma and negative-ion beams, including visible-range CCD imaging and optical tomography.

05

Particle Simulations

Particle-in-Cell modelling of charged-particle systems using IBSimu, with particular interest in self-consistent space-charge effects and three-dimensional beam evolution.

06

Neutral Beam Systems

Modelling of gas neutralization and charge-state evolution of negative-ion beams, including collision processes and residual charged-particle transport.

EXPERIMENTAL

Working with real beams

My experimental work involves plasma and negative-ion beam characterization using electrical and optical diagnostics. I have worked with visible-range CCD systems to obtain transverse beam profiles and investigate spatial variations in extracted negative-ion beams.

I have also developed an optical tomography approach for reconstructing two-dimensional emission distributions from multiple line-of-sight measurements.

COMPUTATIONAL

Understanding the physics through simulation

Computational modelling complements my experimental work by allowing physical mechanisms to be isolated and investigated under controlled conditions.

My simulation work includes Particle-in-Cell studies of space-charge effects, beamlet transport, electrostatic interactions and beam evolution. I also work with Monte Carlo approaches for studying particle collisions and charge-state evolution in neutralizer systems.

RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY

Connecting experiment, simulation and physical insight.

I am particularly interested in research problems where experimental observations and numerical models can be used together to understand an underlying physical mechanism.

Rather than treating experiments and simulations as separate approaches, I see them as complementary tools. Experimental measurements provide physical evidence, while modelling helps reveal the mechanisms responsible for the observed behaviour.

This approach motivates my work on negative-ion beam transport, where space-charge interactions, electromagnetic fields, particle collisions and beam geometry collectively determine the behaviour of the transported beam.
